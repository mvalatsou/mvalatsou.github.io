I am a PhD student in Astrophysics at ETH Zürich, working on the interiors and atmospheres of exoplanets. My research combines interior structure modelling with atmospheric escape and chemical equilibration to understand how the exoplanetary population --- specifically the super-Earths and sub-Neptunes --- has formed and evolved. I am ultimately interested in the diversity of super-Earth atmospheres as a result of their evolution, and also what can make these planets habitable!

Since I work entirely on the modeling and simulation side (no observational work), my day-to-day is largely computational: building physical models in Python, running large parameter simulations, and interpreting the resulting datasets to draw conclusions about planetary environments. Along the way, I've developed a Python-based tool to support this research, which has sharpened my ability to translate physical problems into working code.

I'm increasingly interested in applying my quantitative modeling, simulation, and scientific programming skills beyond academia, particularly in roles at the intersection of physical science and data.
